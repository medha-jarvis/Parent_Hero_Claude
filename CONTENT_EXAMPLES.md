**Purpose:** Show exactly what content is generated for each Campaign topic, what each content type is, who it's for, where it appears in the app, and what the actual generated content looks like.

## Part 2: MATH -- Addition (Single Digit)

**Topic ID:** `g1_m_add_basic`
**Grade:** 1 | **Board:** CBSE | **Subject:** Math
**Campaign Title:** "Addition (Single Digit)"
**5-Day Sub-Topics:** 1. Concept of addition (putting together) → 2. Addition using objects and pictures → 3. Addition facts (sums to 10) → 4. Addition facts (sums to 18) → 5. Addition word problems + assessment

---

### Content Type 1: Teaching Script (Day 1)

> **What it is:** A 3-minute script the parent reads aloud (or uses TTS) to teach the child the topic. Written in a warm, conversational tone. NOT a textbook explanation -- it's a dialogue guide.
>
> **Who it's for:** The parent. The child listens.
>
> **Where it appears:** Campaign Day 1, Step 1. Full-screen reading card with large text. TTS button available.

#### Generated Content:

**ANALOGY:**
- Visual: 🍎🍎 + 🍎🍎🍎 = 🍎🍎🍎🍎🍎
- Text: "Addition is like putting all your toys in one big box!"

**HOOK (Opening -- say this to your child):**
"Ira, show me 2 fingers on your left hand. Now show me 3 fingers on your right hand. Count ALL your fingers together -- how many is that? Five! You just did ADDITION!"

**STEP 1 (2 minutes):**
"Addition means PUTTING TOGETHER. When you have some things, and you get MORE things, and you count them ALL -- that's addition.

Pick up 2 spoons from the kitchen. Now pick up 3 more spoons. Put them all together. Count them. 1... 2... 3... 4... 5! You have 5 spoons!

We write this as: 2 + 3 = 5. The '+' sign means 'put together.' The '=' sign means 'gives us.'"

**STEP 2 (30 seconds):**
"Let's try another one. Take 4 toy cars. Now take 1 more. How many do you have? Count them... 5! So 4 + 1 = 5. Easy!"

**STEP 3 (30 seconds):**
"Now YOU make one up! Pick some objects. Put two groups together. Count them. Tell me the addition sum."

**COMMON PITFALL (for the parent):**
"Children often count one of the groups twice. If they're adding 3 + 2, they might count '1, 2, 3' for the first group, then start again at '1, 2' for the second group and say '2' instead of '5.' Teach them to CONTINUE counting from where the first group ended: '3... 4, 5!'"

---

### Content Type 2: Revised Script (Day 2)

> **What it is:** A shorter (2-minute) script for Day 2, focusing on the pitfall from Day 1 and advancing to the next sub-topic (addition using pictures/objects → towards memorizing facts).
>
> **Who it's for:** Parent.
>
> **Where it appears:** Campaign Day 2, Step 1.

#### Generated Content:

**FOCUS:** Common mistake from Day 1 -- counting both groups from 1.

**KEY POINTS:**

1. **The "count-on" trick:** "Instead of counting both groups, just START from the bigger number and count on. For 5 + 3: put 5 in your head, then count on: 6, 7, 8! Much faster than counting all 8 from the beginning."

2. **Use your fingers as a counter:** "For 4 + 3: hold up 3 fingers. Say '4' in your head, then tap each finger: 5, 6, 7. The last number you say is the answer!"

3. **Draw it:** "If objects aren't handy, draw dots. Two dots and three dots. Count all the dots. Write the sum below."

**DURATION:** 2 minutes

### Content Type 3: Recap Points (Day 3)

> **What it is:** Three quick bullet points the parent reads before the child starts Arcade mode. A 30-second refresher.
>
> **Who it's for:** Parent.
>
> **Where it appears:** Campaign Day 3, Step 1.

#### Generated Content:

1. ➕ means "put together." Two groups become one.
2. Always count ALL the objects -- don't restart the count for the second group.
3. Speed trick: start from the bigger number and count on.

### Content Type 5-7: Quiz Questions (Easy / Medium / Hard)

> **What it is:** Assessment questions used in quizzes and the Day 5 Legend Quiz. Each question is MCQ with 4 options and includes TWO explanation modes.
>
> **Who it's for:** Child answers. Parent reads explanations if child gets it wrong.
>
> **Where it appears:** Campaign Day quizzes, Day 5 Legend Quiz, standalone quiz mode.
>
> **What makes it different from Practice Pad:** Practice Pad = pure number input, speed-focused, no explanations. Quiz = MCQ format, includes explanation on wrong answer, measures understanding.
>
> **Explanation modes:**
> - **"Show Me" (child):** Simple, visual, 1 sentence. Uses emojis and pictures.
> - **"How to Teach This" (parent):** Teaching strategy. 2-3 sentences. Tells parent how to explain the concept.

#### Generated Content (15 Easy, 10 Medium sample, 5 Hard sample):

**EASY (15 questions):**

**Q1:**
- Question: "2 + 1 = ?"
- Options: [2, 3, 4, 1]
- Correct: 3 (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "🍎🍎 + 🍎 = 🍎🍎🍎. Count them: 3!"
- Explanation (parent): "Use physical objects. Put 2 things down, add 1 more, count together. If your child struggles, start with fingers."

**Q2:**
- Question: "What does the '+' sign mean?"
- Options: ["Take away", "Put together", "Equal to", "Minus"]
- Correct: "Put together" (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "➕ means put together! When we add, we combine things into one group."
- Explanation (parent): "This tests conceptual understanding, not computation. Use the phrase 'put together' consistently. Avoid just saying 'plus' without explaining what it means."

**Q3:**
- Question: "3 + 2 = ?"
- Options: [4, 6, 5, 1]
- Correct: 5 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "🍎🍎🍎 + 🍎🍎 = count them all: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5! ✋"
- Explanation (parent): "Show with fingers: hold up 3 on one hand, 2 on the other. Count all together."

**Q4:**
- Question: "Look at the picture: 🐟🐟🐟🐟 + 🐟 = ?"
- Options: [4, 6, 5, 3]
- Correct: 5 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "Count all the fish: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5! There are 5 fish total."
- Explanation (parent): "Image-based addition. Encourage your child to point at each image and count aloud."

**Q5:**
- Question: "Which sum gives 4?"
- Options: ["2 + 3", "1 + 3", "3 + 3", "5 + 1"]
- Correct: "1 + 3" (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "1 + 3 = 4. ☝️ + 🤟 = 4 fingers!"
- Explanation (parent): "This reverses the question -- given the answer, find the sum. This builds number sense. Practise by asking: 'what two numbers make 4?'"

**Q6-Q15:** *(Same format. Mix of computation, picture-based, and conceptual questions. Sums do not exceed 10 for Easy tier.)*

**MEDIUM (10 questions, representative sample):**

**Q16:**
- Question: "Ira has 5 crayons. Her mother gives her 3 more. How many crayons does Ira have now?"
- Options: [7, 8, 2, 9]
- Correct: 8 (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "Ira had 5 ✏️✏️✏️✏️✏️ and got 3 more ✏️✏️✏️. Count them all: 8!"
- Explanation (parent): "Word problem. Help your child identify the two numbers (5 and 3) and the operation (gave more = addition). Underline the key words: 'has 5' and 'gives 3 more.'"

**Q17:**
- Question: "7 + 4 = ?"
- Options: [10, 12, 11, 13]
- Correct: 11 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "Start from 7, count on 4 more: 8, 9, 10, 11! 🎯"
- Explanation (parent): "This crosses the '10 barrier.' Teach the 'count-on' strategy: hold 7 in your head, count up 4 fingers: 8, 9, 10, 11. Or break it: 7 + 3 = 10, then + 1 = 11."

**Q18:**
- Question: "5 + 5 = ?"
- Options: [8, 10, 11, 9]
- Correct: 10 (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "✋ + ✋ = 10! Both your hands together!"
- Explanation (parent): "Doubles (5+5, 4+4, 3+3) are anchor facts. Kids should memorize these first -- they're the fastest path to addition fluency."

**Q19:**
- Question: "Which is MORE: 3 + 4 or 2 + 6?"
- Options: ["3 + 4", "2 + 6", "They are equal", "Can't tell"]
- Correct: "2 + 6" (index 1)
- Explanation (child): "3 + 4 = 7. But 2 + 6 = 8. 8 is more than 7! So 2 + 6 is bigger."
- Explanation (parent): "This tests comparison after computation. Encourage your child to solve BOTH sums first, then compare. Don't let them guess by looking at individual numbers."

**Q20:**
- Question: "6 + __ = 9. What goes in the blank?"
- Options: [2, 4, 3, 5]
- Correct: 3 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "6 + ? = 9. Count from 6: 7, 8, 9 -- that's 3 more! So 6 + 3 = 9."
- Explanation (parent): "Missing addend problems are pre-algebra! The child needs to think: 'what do I add to 6 to reach 9?' The count-on method works perfectly here."

**HARD (5 questions, representative sample):**

**Q21:**
- Question: "There are 8 children in a bus. At the next stop, 7 more get in. How many children are in the bus now?"
- Options: [14, 16, 15, 13]
- Correct: 15 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "8 children + 7 more children = 15 children on the bus. Start at 8, count 7 more: 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15!"
- Explanation (parent): "Sums approaching 18 are challenging for Grade 1. If your child struggles, break it into parts: 8 + 7 = 8 + 2 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15. This 'making 10' strategy is powerful."

**Q22:**
- Question: "3 + 4 + 2 = ?"
- Options: [8, 7, 9, 10]
- Correct: 9 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "Add two at a time! 3 + 4 = 7. Then 7 + 2 = 9."
- Explanation (parent): "Three-addend problems. Teach your child to add the first two numbers, then add the third to that sum. Using fingers helps: 3 fingers + 4 fingers = 7, then 2 more = 9."

**Q23:**
- Question: "Ira had some stickers. She got 4 more and now she has 9. How many did she start with?"
- Options: [4, 6, 5, 3]
- Correct: 5 (index 2)
- Explanation (child): "? + 4 = 9. Count back from 9: 8, 7, 6, 5. She started with 5!"
- Explanation (parent): "This is an inverse problem -- essentially subtraction disguised as addition. It tests deep understanding. Ask: 'what number plus 4 gives 9?' Use objects to work backward."

### Content Type 9: Beat the Parent Quiz

> **What it is:** 10 questions that BOTH the parent and child take (asynchronously). Results are compared. Meant to be fun and bonding, not stressful.
>
> **Who it's for:** Parent takes it first. Then child takes the same 10 questions. App compares scores.
>
> **Where it appears:** Campaign Day 4.
>
> **Design principle:** Questions should be slightly tricky so parents don't always get 10/10. A mix of easy sums and sneaky word problems.

#### Generated Content:

| # | Question | Options | Correct | Why It's Tricky |
|---|----------|---------|---------|----------------|
| 1 | 8 + 5 = ? | [12, 13, 14, 11] | 13 | Straightforward but crosses 10 |
| 2 | Which is bigger: 7 + 6 or 8 + 4? | [7+6, 8+4, Same, Can't tell] | 7+6 (=13 vs 12) | Requires solving both |
| 3 | 0 + 7 = ? | [0, 7, 70, 1] | 7 | Identity property -- "0" option is a trap |
| 4 | Ira has 6 toys. Rohan has 5 toys. How many MORE does Ira have? | [11, 1, 2, 3] | 1 | Trap: most will add (11). Question asks "how many MORE" = subtraction concept |
| 5 | 9 + __ = 15. What is the missing number? | [5, 7, 6, 8] | 6 | Missing addend |
| 6 | 5 + 4 = 4 + ? | [4, 5, 9, 3] | 5 | Tests commutativity (a+b = b+a) |
| 7 | How many addition sums give exactly 10? (Choose: 5+5, 7+4, 6+4, 8+2) | [2, 3, 4, 1] | 3 (5+5, 6+4, 8+2) | 7+4=11 is the fake |
| 8 | A farmer has 9 hens and 8 chicks. How many birds in total? | [16, 17, 18, 15] | 17 | Large sum; "birds" requires understanding hens and chicks are both birds |
| 9 | True or false: 6 + 3 = 3 + 6 | [True, False] | True | Commutative property |
| 10 | Priya ate 3 chapatis for lunch and 2 for dinner. Next day she ate 4 for lunch. How many chapatis total in both days? | [9, 5, 7, 6] | 9 | Multi-step: 3+2+4. Trap: only adding one day's total |

### Content Type 10-14: Daily Spark Content

> **What it is:** One piece of delightful, bite-sized content delivered daily via push notification. Each day of the week has a different content type. The content is tied to the active Campaign topic.
>
> **Who it's for:** Varies by type (see below).
>
> **Where it appears:** Push notification → opens in-app content card.

#### Daily Spark: Riddles (2 generated, used on Mondays)

> **What it is:** A short, topic-related riddle that arrives as a push notification.
> **Who it's for:** Child (parent can help).
> **App behavior:** Notification shows the riddle. Child thinks. Taps notification to open the app and see the answer.

**Riddle 1:**
"I'm less than 10 but more than 5. I'm what you get when you add 3 and 4. What number am I?"
**Answer:** "7! Because 3 + 4 = 7. 🎯"

**Riddle 2:**
"Add me to myself and you get 10. I'm the number of fingers on one hand. What am I?"
**Answer:** "5! Because 5 + 5 = 10. ✋"

#### Daily Spark: Fun Facts (2 generated, used on Tuesdays)

> **What it is:** A surprising or delightful fact related to the topic. Delivered entirely within the push notification (no need to open app).
> **Who it's for:** Child and parent.
> **Purpose:** Delight + topic awareness. Not a teaching moment.

**Fun Fact 1:**
"Did you know? Ancient people used PEBBLES to add numbers. The word 'calculate' comes from 'calculus' -- the Latin word for PEBBLE! 🪨"

**Fun Fact 2:**
"Your brain can add small numbers in 0.3 seconds -- that's faster than you can blink! 🧠⚡"

#### Daily Spark: Mini Challenges (3 questions, used on Wednesdays)

> **What it is:** 3 rapid-fire questions the child answers in the app. Quick, fun, low-stakes.
> **Who it's for:** Child.
> **App behavior:** Notification says "Quick Challenge! 3 questions in 60 seconds. Can you do it?" Opens a simplified quiz screen.

**Challenge 1:** "5 + 4 = ?" → 9
**Challenge 2:** "What number + 3 = 8?" → 5
**Challenge 3:** "Is 7 + 2 the same as 2 + 7? Yes or No?" → Yes

#### Daily Spark: Stories (2 generated, used on Thursdays)

> **What it is:** A very short (4-5 sentence) story that weaves in the topic. Meant to be read together (parent reads, child listens).
> **Who it's for:** Parent reads to child.
> **App behavior:** Opens an illustrated reading card (illustration = static themed image).

**Story 1: "Rohan's Marble Jar"**
"Rohan had a big glass jar. Every day, he put marbles in it. On Monday, he put 3 red marbles. On Tuesday, he put 4 blue marbles. 'How many marbles do I have now?' he asked his sister. She counted: 3 + 4 = 7! 'Seven marbles!' she shouted. Rohan smiled. By Friday, he wanted to guess how many there would be. Can YOU help Rohan count?"

**Story 2: "The Counting Ants"**
"Five little ants were carrying crumbs to their home. Three more ants came to help. 'How many are we now?' asked the littlest ant. 'Five plus three is eight!' said the biggest ant. 'Eight ants is a STRONG team!' And together, they carried the biggest crumb home."

#### Daily Spark: Activities (2 generated, used on Fridays)

> **What it is:** A hands-on physical activity the parent and child do together. Uses only household items.
> **Who it's for:** Parent sets up, child participates.
> **App behavior:** Opens an activity card with step-by-step instructions.

**Activity 1: "Dice Addition Game"**
- **You need:** 2 dice (or a dice app on your phone), paper, pencil.
- **How to play:**
  1. Roll both dice.
  2. Add the two numbers (e.g., 4 and 3 = 7).
  3. Write the sum on paper: 4 + 3 = 7.
  4. Take turns. Play 10 rounds.
  5. Whoever has the highest sum total wins!
- **Learning:** Speed addition practice in a fun, competitive format.
- **Time:** 10 minutes.

**Activity 2: "Snack Addition"**
- **You need:** Small snacks (raisins, grapes, biscuits, etc.)
- **How to play:**
  1. Give your child 5 raisins on one plate.
  2. Give them 3 raisins on another plate.
  3. Ask: "If you put them all together, how many will you have?"
  4. Let them combine and count: 5 + 3 = 8!
  5. They get to eat the answer! 😋
  6. Repeat with different numbers.
- **Learning:** Physical manipulation of objects reinforces addition as "combining."
- **Time:** 10 minutes.

## Part 3: SCIENCE/EVS -- Plants Around Us

**Topic ID:** `g1_s_plants`

**5-Day Sub-Topics:** 1. Parts of a plant (root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit) → 2. Types of plants (trees, shrubs, herbs, climbers) → 3. What plants need (water, sunlight, soil, air) → 4. Plants give us (food, shade, oxygen) → 5. Taking care of plants + assessment

### Teaching Script (Day 1)

**ANALOGY:**
- Visual: 🌱 → 🌿 → 🌳
- Text: "A plant is like a family -- every part has a job, just like every family member!"

**HOOK:**
"Go to the window or balcony. Point at a plant. Can you see its leaves? Can you see its stem? Today, we're going to learn the SECRET JOBS of every part of a plant!"

**STEP 1 (1.5 minutes -- Parts):**
"A plant has 5 main parts. Let's learn them from bottom to top:

🌱 **ROOTS** -- Hidden underground! They drink water from the soil, like a straw. They also hold the plant so it doesn't fall over.
🟤 **STEM** -- The tall part. It's like an elevator -- it carries water from the roots UP to the leaves.
🍃 **LEAVES** -- The kitchen! Leaves use sunlight to cook food for the plant. (We'll learn HOW later!)
🌸 **FLOWER** -- The beautiful part! But flowers aren't just pretty. They help the plant make SEEDS so new plants can grow.
🍎 **FRUIT** -- The package! Fruits carry the seeds inside them. When you eat an apple, the seeds are inside!"

**STEP 2 (1 minute -- Touch and feel):**
"Now, go find a real plant (in a pot or garden). Touch each part:
- Can you feel the soil? The roots are hiding under there.
- Touch the stem. Is it hard or soft?
- Touch a leaf. Is it smooth or rough?
- Can you see any flowers or fruits?"

**STEP 3 (30 seconds):**
"Draw a plant and label all 5 parts: Root, Stem, Leaf, Flower, Fruit. Use different colors!"

**COMMON PITFALL:**
"Children often confuse 'stem' with 'trunk.' For small plants, it's a stem. For trees, the thick wooden part is called a trunk (which is a type of stem). Don't overcomplicate it at this stage -- 'stem' is fine for all."

---

### Revised Script (Day 2)

**FOCUS:** Types of plants -- size-based classification.

**KEY POINTS:**

1. **Trees:** Very tall, thick trunk, many branches. "Look at a neem tree or mango tree. It's taller than your building! That's a tree."

2. **Shrubs:** Shorter than trees, many stems from the ground, bushy. "The roses in the park? Those bushes are shrubs."

3. **Herbs:** Very small, soft green stems, often used in cooking. "Mint, tulsi (holy basil), coriander -- these are herbs. You can easily pull them out of the ground."

4. **Climbers & Creepers:** Need support. Climbers go UP (money plant, grapevine). Creepers spread ALONG the ground (pumpkin, watermelon).

---

### Recap Points (Day 3)

1. 🌿 Plants have 5 parts: Root (drinks water), Stem (carries water up), Leaf (makes food), Flower (makes seeds), Fruit (carries seeds).
2. 🌳 Trees are tall with trunks. Shrubs are bushy. Herbs are small and soft.
3. 💧 Plants need water, sunlight, soil, and air to grow.

## Part 4: ENGLISH -- Naming Words (Nouns)

**Topic ID:** `g1_e_nouns`
**Grade:** 1 | **Board:** CBSE | **Subject:** English
**Campaign Title:** "Naming Words (Nouns)"
**5-Day Sub-Topics:** 1. What is a naming word? → 2. People nouns → Animal nouns → 3. Place nouns → Thing nouns → 4. Nouns in sentences (identify the noun) → 5. Mixed practice + assessment

---

### Teaching Script (Day 1)

**ANALOGY:**
- Visual: 👩 = "mother" | 🐕 = "dog" | 🏫 = "school" | 📖 = "book"
- Text: "A naming word is the NAME of anything in the world -- every single thing has a name!"

**HOOK:**
"Ira, look around. What do you see? A table? A fan? A window? Now, what do you call your teacher at school? And what do you call that furry animal that says 'woof'? ALL of those are NAMING WORDS. Today, you're going to become a NAMING WORD detective! 🔍"

**STEP 1 (1.5 minutes):**
"Every person has a name. Every animal has a name. Every place has a name. Every thing has a name. These names are called NAMING WORDS -- or, the fancy English word: NOUNS.

Let's sort them into 4 groups:

👩👦 **People:** mother, father, teacher, doctor, boy, girl, baby
🐕🐱 **Animals:** dog, cat, lion, parrot, fish, butterfly
🏫🏠 **Places:** school, home, park, market, hospital, playground
📖✏️ **Things:** book, pen, chair, ball, clock, phone

See? EVERYTHING around you has a name. And those names are nouns!"

**STEP 2 (1 minute):**
"Let's play a game. I'll point to something, and you tell me its naming word.
- *Point to a chair* → 'Chair!' (That's a naming word!)
- *Point to yourself* → 'Mummy/Papa!' (That's a naming word too!)
- *Point outside to a tree* → 'Tree!' (Naming word!)

Your turn -- point to 5 things and say their naming words."

**STEP 3 (30 seconds):**
"Remember: if you can SEE it, TOUCH it, HEAR it, or THINK about it -- and it has a name -- it's a NOUN!"

**COMMON PITFALL:**
"Children sometimes confuse naming words with describing words or action words. 'Big' is NOT a noun (it describes). 'Run' is NOT a noun (it's an action). A noun is always the NAME of a thing. Test: can you put 'a' or 'the' before it? 'A dog' ✓ (noun). 'A big' ✗ (not a noun). 'A run' ✗ (not a noun)."

---

### Revised Script (Day 2)

**FOCUS:** Deepening with the 4 categories + common confusion with action words.

**KEY POINTS:**

1. **The "Can I put 'a' before it?" test:** "'A table' -- yes, table is a noun. 'A running' -- no, that sounds wrong, so running is NOT a noun."

2. **People nouns include jobs:** "Doctor, teacher, police, farmer -- these are ALL naming words because they name a TYPE of person."

3. **The difference:** "'The dog RUN' -- 'dog' is the naming word (who?), 'runs' is the doing word (what does the dog do?). They're partners but NOT the same thing."

---

### Recap Points (Day 3)

1. 📛 A noun is the NAME of a person, animal, place, or thing.
2. 🧪 Test: Can you put "a" or "the" before it? Then it's a noun!
3. 🔍 Nouns answer the question "WHO?" or "WHAT?"
